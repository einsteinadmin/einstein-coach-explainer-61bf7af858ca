TRAINING CAMP COACHES ROOM

How Coach Works

Coach is Claude, sitting in one Slack channel, reading one folder of documents that we wrote. That folder is the whole brain. Nothing else.

For Cameron · rev. 9/7/2026 pm · day two of the pilot

The short version

IN ONE BREATHTag @Claude with a question. It opens the playbook, finds the pages that match, and answers you with a date on the answer. If the playbook does not cover it, it says so and tells you who to ask. It never guesses, and it cannot change anything.

Think of a binder in the break room. Coach is the guy who has read the whole binder and never gets tired of being asked. He is not allowed to make things up, and he is not allowed to write in the binder. When the binder is wrong, a person fixes the binder.

That last part is the whole design. Everything below is about how pages get into the binder, and who is allowed to put them there.

Why the dates matter

Coach reads the folder fresh every single time somebody asks. Change a file at noon and the 12:05 answer uses the new version. There is no training, no memory, no learning. That is on purpose: it means the only way to change what Coach says is to change the playbook, and every change has a name on it.

When Coach doesn't know

This is the part you asked about. Here is exactly what happens, and where you go to see it.

Coach says "that's not in the playbook yet," names the person who owns that area, and stops. It does not guess, and it does not write itself a note, because it cannot write anything.

So I keep the note. There is now a file called playbook-gaps.md. I read the channel and add one row for every question Coach could not answer: what was asked, what was missing, who owns it, and whether it is closed. Mike, Brian, and Anne each read that one file to see their own backlog. Monday's injury question is the first row, already closed.

I sweep the channel every morning for the first two weeks, then weekly. A thumbs down on a bad answer gets swept too.

Who does Coach tag?

RULED 9/7When Coach knows the answer, it tags nobody. It just answers. When the playbook does not cover it, it @-mentions the owner right there in the thread, so the person who can close the gap actually sees it. That is the whole rule, and it is live now.

The routing table decides who gets the mention. App questions go to Mike. Training day and the Central Texas and Houston branches go to Brian. Hiring, the handbook, trial days, and anything HR go to Anne. Data and systems go to Matisen.

Two things never get a mention. A question about a specific mover's performance, pay, discipline or health, which goes to a regional manager or Anne with no ping and no detail. And anything about promotion, which goes to Anne in plain text until the framework is published.

How an answer gets into the playbook

Today it takes a human hand at step 4. That is the step the pull request would remove.

  1. Somebody hits a wall

    A question with no answer in the playbook. anybody

  2. Coach says so and names the owner

    The gap goes on my sweep list. coach

  3. The owner decides what the rule should be

    Right there in the channel, in their own words. Mike for the app, Brian for training days, Anne for people. the owner

  4. Coach writes it up properly

    It turns the ruling into the exact wording for the decisions log and for whichever file needs the new section. coach

  5. Somebody puts it in the folder

    Today that means opening the file on GitHub and pasting. This is the step we are removing, see below. What never gets removed is a person approving it. a human approves, always

  6. It gets a status

    Decided but not announced means Coach will not quote it yet, it points people to the owner. Once the owner tells the team, the status flips and Coach starts using it. the owner

↺

The next person who asks that question gets a real answer, with a date on it. That is the loop. Every gap someone hits makes the playbook permanently better for everyone behind them.

Getting rid of the paste step

Nobody should have to open GitHub and paste text to fix a sentence. Two ways out, and they are not exclusive.

Option A: Coach files the change itself

FEWEST HANDS · NEEDS ONE RULE CHANGED

Coach writes the change as a proposal that sits in the folder until somebody approves it. Mike or you tap approve from your phone. Nothing lands without that tap.

What it costs: Coach's instructions currently forbid it from writing anything at all, so that rule gets narrowed rather than deleted. The access is already there.

Best when the ruling happened in the channel and everybody watching should see the change that came out of it.

Option B: Gerald makes the change for Mike

NO NEW HABITS · MIKE ALREADY WORKS THIS WAY

Coach spots the gap and drafts the wording. Mike tells Gerald to put it in the training camp playbook. Gerald edits and pushes. Mike is already having this exact conversation with Gerald every day about his own repo.

What it costs: Gerald needs access to the playbook folder, which is one invite. Coach stays strictly read-only, which is a cleaner line to hold.

Best when the change is bigger than one paragraph, or Mike wants to think it through before it is visible to the room.

THE PART THAT DOES NOT CHANGEWhichever mechanic we use, a person approves before it counts, and every change arrives marked decided-but-not-announced so approving it never accidentally announces it to the field. The mechanic is a preference. The approval is the rule.

When a change gets proposed at all

Narrow, or the folder fills with noise. One trigger:

  • ✓The owner of that area states a rule, in the channel, in response to a gap. Mike on the app. Brian on training days. Anne on people. That is it.
  • ✗Not when somebody asks a question.
  • ✗Not when somebody shares an opinion or an idea.
  • ✗Not when somebody rules outside their lane. A branch manager cannot rule on company policy.
  • ✗Never anything about a specific person, their pay, or discipline.
