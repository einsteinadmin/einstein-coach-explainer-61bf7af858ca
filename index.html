How Coach Works

Coach is Claude, sitting in one Slack channel, reading one folder of documents that we wrote. That folder is the whole brain. Nothing else.

For Cameron and the leadership team · rev. 9/7/2026 · design ruled with Mike

The short version

IN ONE BREATHTag @Claude with a question. It opens the playbook, finds the pages that match, and answers you with a date on the answer. If the playbook does not cover it, it says so and tells you who to ask. It never guesses, and it cannot change anything.

Think of a binder in the break room. Coach is the guy who has read the whole binder and never gets tired of being asked. He is not allowed to make things up, and he is not allowed to write in the binder. When the binder is wrong, a person fixes the binder.

That last part is the whole design. Everything below is about how pages get into the binder, and who is allowed to put them there.

Why the dates matter

Coach reads the folder fresh every single time somebody asks. Change a file at noon and the 12:05 answer uses the new version. There is no training, no memory, no learning. That is on purpose: it means the only way to change what Coach says is to change the playbook, and every change has a name on it.

When Coach doesn't know

This is the part you asked about. Here is exactly what happens, and where you go to see it.

Coach says "that's not in the playbook yet." Then it sorts the gap into one of two kinds, because they close in completely different ways. This split is Mike's, and it is the part of the design I had wrong.

A write-down gapcloses in minutes

The rule already exists, or the app already does the thing. Nobody wrote it down. Coach asks the owner, proposes the exact wording, and on their confirmation puts it in the playbook itself.

A feature requestgoes on a board

The app genuinely cannot do it. Coach captures what was asked, and on the owner's confirmation puts one pulse in their inbox with the request, who asked, and a link to the thread. It never designs the feature.

If Coach cannot tell which kind it is, it asks one question before choosing.

Either way the gap gets logged in playbook-gaps.md, so anyone can see what is still open and who owns it. I sweep the channel on top of that to catch anything Coach missed and anything that got a thumbs down.

Who does Coach tag?

RULED 9/7When Coach knows the answer, it tags nobody. It just answers. When the playbook does not cover it, it @-mentions the owner right there in the thread, so the person who can close the gap actually sees it. That is the whole rule, and it is live now.

The routing table decides who gets the mention. App questions go to Mike. Training day and the Central Texas and Houston branches go to Brian. Hiring, the handbook, trial days, and anything HR go to Anne. Data and systems go to Matisen.

Two things never get a mention. A question about a specific mover's performance, pay, discipline or health, which goes to a regional manager or Anne with no ping and no detail. And anything about promotion, which goes to Anne in plain text until the framework is published.

How an answer gets into the playbook

Today it takes a human hand at step 4. That is the step the pull request would remove.

  1. Somebody hits a wall

    A question with no answer in the playbook. anybody

  2. Coach says so and names the owner

    The gap goes on my sweep list. coach

  3. The owner decides what the rule should be

    Right there in the channel, in their own words. Mike for the app, Brian for training days, Anne for people. the owner

  4. Coach writes it up properly

    It turns the ruling into the exact wording for the decisions log and for whichever file needs the new section. coach

  5. Coach writes it, once somebody confirms

    Coach posts the exact wording, the owner confirms in the thread, and Coach makes the change. Nobody opens GitHub. What never goes away is a person confirming first. a human confirms, always

  6. It gets a status

    Decided but not announced means Coach will not quote it yet, it points people to the owner. Once the owner tells the team, the status flips and Coach starts using it. the owner

The next person who asks that question gets a real answer, with a date on it. That is the loop. Every gap someone hits makes the playbook permanently better for everyone behind them.

How Coach closes a gap

Ruled 9/7 with Mike. The old version ended with somebody opening GitHub and pasting text. That step is gone.

Lane 1: the write-down gap

COACH WRITES IT · THE OWNER CONFIRMS FIRST

Coach tags the owner and asks them the actual question. When they answer, Coach writes out the exact words it would put in the playbook, quoted, naming every file it would touch. Then it asks them to confirm.

On their confirmation, Coach makes the change itself: edits the file, logs the ruling as decided-but-not-announced, closes the gap row, and posts back what it changed. If they reword it, their wording wins.

Lane 2: the feature request

COACH CAPTURES IT · THE OWNER CONFIRMS FIRST

Coach says plainly that the app cannot do this today, then writes back what it understood the person is asking for and invites a correction. It offers to put it on the owner's board.

On their confirmation it creates one pulse in their inbox: the request in the requester's own words, who asked and from which branch, and a link to the thread. It checks for an existing pulse first, so the same idea from three branches does not become three pulses, and it leaves the date and effort estimate empty because those are the owner's call at triage.

The four things that keep this safe

  • 1The exact text, never a summary. Approving a description of a change is not the same as approving the change. Coach pastes the real words, so yes means yes to those words.
  • 2Only the person Coach tagged can confirm. If a branch manager says "yeah do it" on a question aimed at Mike, that does not count and Coach waits.
  • 3Coach never designs the feature. It writes down what was asked and invites a correction. No spec, no mechanics, no effort estimate. Capturing a request, not solving it.
  • 4No standing permission. Every write needs a fresh confirmation on that specific proposal. Approving one last week approves nothing this week.

EVERY PULSE SIGNS ITSELFCoach is running on Cameron's Monday credential, so a pulse it creates would otherwise look like Cameron made it. So every one ends with a line naming Coach and the channel it came from, who requested it, who confirmed it, and a reminder that capturing a request is not a promise to build it. Nobody has to guess where a pulse came from or who asked for it.

WHAT COACH CAN WRITE TOTwo places, and nothing else ever: this playbook, and a new pulse on a leader's board. It never edits another repo, never changes or closes an existing pulse, never posts in another channel, and never sends anyone a message. And everything it writes to the playbook lands marked not-yet-announced, so a rule existing is never the same thing as the field having heard it.

How this cascades down

  1. Leadership, now

    The Roundtable is in and has been testing it. Mike ran the only real gap so far and shaped the design that came out of it. running

  2. All ten branch managers

    The actual audience. They are the ones who hit questions nobody wrote down, every day. Once they approve of it, the next group goes in. next

  3. Ops managers and flex managers

    The layer under the branch managers, and the one most likely to be guessing at a rule rather than asking. after the BMs sign off

  4. The branch channels, where the movers are

    A mover asks in their own branch channel and gets the same answer their manager would get. That is the point of building it this way. the real destination
